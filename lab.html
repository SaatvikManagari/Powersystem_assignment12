The method

The technique classifies all eleven conditions (no fault, AG, BG, CG, AB, BC, CA, ABG, BCG, CAG, ABC) from one cycle of the three phase currents measured at one line end. It relies only on classical fault-analysis theory: symmetrical components, the sequence-network connections of each fault, and the Thevenin / pure-fault superposition.

1 · Test system and simulation

The test system is a two-source line: G1 – T1 (YNyn) – line – T2 (YNd) – G2. A fault at a fraction x of the line from bus 2 splits each sequence network into a left and a right part. The Thevenin impedance at the fault in sequence s is

$$Z_{s,th}=\frac{Z_{s,\text{left}}\,Z_{s,\text{right}}}{Z_{s,\text{left}}+Z_{s,\text{right}}},\qquad Z_{0,\text{right}}=(1-x)Z_{0L}+Z_{0T2}$$

The zero-sequence right side stops at T2: its delta winding shorts zero-sequence current to the reference and isolates G2. Instead of a separate formula per fault, every shunt fault is written as a phase-domain fault admittance matrix Yf (Rf to ground, between phases, or two phases joined then Rf to ground). The Thevenin equation becomes

$$\big(\mathbf I + Z^{abc}_{th}Y_f\big)\,V^{abc}_F = V^{abc}_{F,\text{pre}},\qquad Z^{abc}_{th}=T\,\mathrm{diag}(Z_0,Z_1,Z_2)\,T^{-1},\qquad I^{abc}_f = Y_f V^{abc}_F$$

The Verification tab checks this solver against the closed-form analytical results (for example \(I_{a1}=E/(Z_1+Z_2+Z_0+3Z_f)\) for LG) and against a worked Z-bus example.

The relay current is the pre-fault load current plus the pure-fault (superimposed) current. That current comes from exciting the dead network at F with the fault current, split between the two sides:

$$\Delta I_{s,\text{relay}} = I_{s,f}\,\frac{Z_{s,\text{right}}}{Z_{s,\text{left}}+Z_{s,\text{right}}},\qquad \Delta V_{s,\text{bus2}} = -Z_{s,\text{src}}\,\Delta I_{s,\text{relay}}$$

Waveforms are sampled at 64 samples/cycle. The fault current includes the decaying DC offset, \(i=i_s+i_t\), with \(i_t(t)=-[i_s(t_0)-i_{\text{pre}}(t_0)]\,e^{-(R/L)(t-t_0)}\), so the current stays continuous at the inception instant. White Gaussian noise at a chosen SNR is added to every channel.

2 · Classification algorithm

  1. Detection. One-cycle superimposed sample \(d[k]=|i[k]-i[k-N]|\). Fault declared when \(d\) exceeds \(5\times\) its pre-fault rms (or 5 % of the pre-fault peak) on any phase for 4 consecutive samples.
  2. Phasors. Full-cycle DFT on a pre-fault window (ending ¼ cycle before detection) and on the first full cycle after detection.
  3. Superimposed currents. \(\Delta I_{abc}=I_{abc}^{\text{fault}}-I_{abc}^{\text{pre}}\). This removes the load current. It is exactly the pure-fault network.
  4. Sequence components. \([\Delta I_0\;\Delta I_1\;\Delta I_2]^T=T^{-1}\Delta I_{abc}\).
  5. Decision. Three features:
    • \(r_2=|\Delta I_2|/|\Delta I_1|<0.2\) → balanced: ABC.
    • \(r_0=|\Delta I_0|/|\Delta I_1|>\max(0.01,\;4\sigma_0/|\Delta I_1|)\) → ground involved. \(\sigma_0\) is the noise level of the zero-sequence phasor, estimated from the pre-fault data.
    • \(\theta=\angle(\Delta I_2/\Delta I_1)\) picks the faulted phases from six 60° sectors.

3 · Why the angle θ identifies the phases

With phase a as reference, the fault boundary conditions fix the ratio \(I_{a2}/I_{a1}\) at the fault.

  • AG: sequence networks in series, so \(I_{a2}=I_{a1}\) and θ = 0°.
  • BG: the same relation holds in phase-b reference. Rotating back gives \(I_{a1}=\alpha I_{b1}\) and \(I_{a2}=\alpha^2 I_{b1}\), so θ = +120°.
  • BC (LL): positive and negative networks in parallel, so \(I_{a2}=-I_{a1}\) and θ = 180°.
  • BCG: \(I_{a2}=-I_{a1}\,(Z_0+3Z_f)/(Z_2+Z_0+3Z_f)\), so θ ≈ 180°.

Positive and negative sequence networks have almost identical impedances, so the relay sees essentially the same ratio as the fault point.

Each class owns a ±30° sector, so the method tolerates large angle errors from noise, fault resistance, and the DC offset.

4 · Known limits

  • ABC and ABCG look the same to this method because both have no zero-sequence current. They are reported as one class.
  • LLG faults with very large Rf (above about 500 Ω on this 220 kV system) carry almost no zero-sequence current and start being read as LL.
  • Series (open-conductor) faults, evolving faults and CT saturation are not modelled.
